## Scope and reviewed exceptions

Reviewed all assigned single-character candidate lists, and compound-only
syllables, before authoring the lists. Ordinary neutral surface forms matching
a lexical syllable are covered by the existing neutral-reading consistency
rule, not added indiscriminately to flashcards. Explicit lexical neutral entries
such as 地 de, 着 zhe, 呢 ne and 头 tou remain included.

Included less-obvious documented readings: 和 hé/hè/hú/huó/huò, 打 dá/dǎ,
女 rǔ (old 汝 variant), 听 yǐn (archaic smile), 离 chī (archaic beast),
无 mó, 哪 né/něi, 车 jū, 行 héng, 同 tòng, 亲 qìng and surname 任 rén.
Compound review adds 大 dá (大鳞大麻哈鱼), 家 gū (阿家), 间 gān
(半间不界), 爸 bǎ and 比 bí (爸比), 比 bì (第比利斯), and 谁 shuí
(also explicitly mentioned in the single-character entry, plus 阿谁).

Excluded the non-syllabic 儿 `r5` dictionary suffix: it is not a standalone
syllable representable by the repo's one-syllable-per-Hanzi convention. 儿 has
lexical ér; a syllabic neutral er is accepted by the existing neutral rule.
Workers should avoid constructions that would require a separate non-syllabic
token pronunciation. Excluded 谁 sei (dialectal 没谁了) and 工 ei in 工口
(a visual Japanese-katakana loan rather than an ordinary Mandarin 工 reading).
Region-only accent alternatives noted parenthetically in entries, e.g. 听
tìng and 行 xìng, are outside this standard-lexical list's scope. These choices
do not claim every dialect/orthographic variant is covered.

These are authored reference-based reading lists, not contextual pronunciation
approval of stories that do not yet exist. Workers must choose contextual
readings, consult references and self-review their own stories. Checker passes
will establish mechanical consistency only, not completeness or language quality.
